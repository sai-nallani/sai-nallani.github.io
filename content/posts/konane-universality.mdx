---
title: "Universality and Konane"
date: "2024-12-23"
---

*Every short combinatorial game value can be realized by a Konane position.*

Konane is a Hawaiian jumping game. The board starts full, with black and white stones in a checkerboard pattern. In the opening, each player removes one stone from the board. After this, a player moves by taking one of their stones and jumping orthogonally over an opposing stone into an empty square, capturing it. Whoever makes the last move wins. 

Conway builds every game inductively by *birthday*: 0 is born on day 0, and each game on a later day is made from games born before it. Games with finite game trees are called *short*, and their values form the short Conway group.

Most rulesets only reach a corner of that group. Red-Blue Hackenbush positions are all surreal numbers, Nim positions are all nimbers, and Domineering reaches switches, numbers and nimbers. A ruleset is a *habitat* for a set of values if every value in the set appears as some position, and it is *universal* if it is a habitat for every short game. Is any ruleset universal?

Carvalho and Santos proved that Konane is universal. The paper works through their proof: it first builds integers, dyadic fractions and nimbers out of Konane positions, then introduces two gadgets, *rubber bands* and *taps*, and uses them to prove the main theorem.
