*A paper and a talk, given at Euler Circle, that build measure theory from scratch and end at the Radon–Nikodym theorem.*

## Why measure theory

The Riemann integral only handles functions that are well behaved everywhere except on sets of "measure zero", which raises the question of what measure should mean in the first place. Through the late nineteenth century, Cantor, Peano, Jordan and Borel each tried to pin down what the "size" of a set should be, and in 1902 Lebesgue's dissertation turned those ideas into a rigorous theory of measure and a far more general integral. In the 1930s, Radon and Nikodym described exactly when one measure can be written as a density against another.

## What's inside

The paper starts with the extended real line and σ-algebras, then builds the Borel σ-algebra, outer measure and Lebesgue measure. It compares the Riemann and Lebesgue integrals, proves the limit theorems including Monotone Convergence, and moves on to signed and complex measures and the Hahn decomposition.

All of this leads to the Radon–Nikodym theorem. The paper closes with two places it shows up in probability: probability density functions and conditional expectation.
